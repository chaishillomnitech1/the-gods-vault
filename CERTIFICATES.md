## What a certificate is

Each piece is a one-of-one master. Its certificate states the piece, its
edition, its SHA-256 hash (the fingerprint of the exact file), its size,
its date, and its QR code. The buyer receives the digital art file + this
certificate + the QR. Anyone can verify: hash the file they received and
compare it to the hash on the certificate. If the hashes match, the piece
is authentic. If they don't, it isn't.

**"Museum grade" is the artist's designation** — Chais The Great grades
his own pieces museum grade. No outside museum has graded them, and none
is claimed.

**Signature:** each certificate carries the artist's name — Chais The Great
— as the signature of authorship. His hand signature is his to add to any
certificate; the record below is the authorship as documented.

## How a buyer verifies (3 steps)

1. Scan the QR on the certificate — it reads the piece title, the edition
   (1 of 1), the full SHA-256, and the verification page.
2. Hash the art file received (`sha256sum <file>` or any hash tool).
3. Compare. Match = authentic, one of one. No match = not the piece.

## Identity rule — a color change does not duplicate a piece

A piece is its code + its number, not its pixels. If a piece is recolored,
cropped, or resized, it is still the same piece — same tracking number,
same code, same certificate. A copy with a different code is not the piece,
and no second certificate is ever issued for the same code. A new piece
gets a new number. That is what makes duplication impossible: the identity
lives in the code, not in the colors.

## The sealed element — purchaser only

Each certificate carries one sealed element — the detail that makes that
piece special beyond the art itself. It is printed ONLY on the purchaser's
certificate. It appears nowhere public: not on this registry, not on the
storefront, not on Pinterest, not in GitHub. The purchaser chooses whether
to highlight and reveal it, or keep it sealed — the choice is theirs, and
theirs alone.

The sealed elements live in a separate private record —
`~/workspace/income/certificates-reveal-keys-2026-11-19.md` — marked DO
NOT PUBLISH and DO NOT PUSH TO GITHUB. When a piece sells, its element
goes on that buyer's certificate alone. Until then, only the artist and
Muse hold them.
